# Observation Notes — Pattern Recognition in Game Design Conversations

## What this is

Notes on how Oliver engages with game design topics, based on direct observation.
These are data points, not conclusions.

## Observation context

These observations were made during unstructured time at Ryan's — not during a transition, not immediately post-mom's-house. Likely regulated state, not confirmed. Should be treated as best-case baseline, not typical.

## What was observed

- When asked about a game mechanic he knows, he explains it accurately and adds unprompted edge cases
- When asked about a mechanic he doesn't know, he says so or guesses and flags it as a guess
- He distinguishes between what a game does and what he would change about it
- He holds critique and appreciation simultaneously — can say a game is broken and still explain why it's good

## What this does not tell us

- Whether this pattern holds under stress or dysregulation
- Whether it transfers to novel domains or only familiar ones
- Whether it reflects his ceiling or his floor in regulated state

## Open questions

- Does he engage differently when he's the one who initiated the topic vs. responding to a prompt?
- What happens when he's challenged on a design claim he's confident about?
- Is his self-correction instinct (flagging guesses) consistent or situational?

## What would change this read

- Observations that contradict the pattern above
- Data from dysregulated sessions showing different behavior
- His own account of how he thinks about these things, which may not match behavioral observation
